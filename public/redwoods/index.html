    The Laurelhurst walk

    Laurelhurst Park grows all three surviving members of the redwood subfamily along one stroll — giant sequoias, coast redwoods, and deciduous dawn redwoods, a species known only from fossils until living trees were found in China in the 1940s. Start near SE Oak & César E. Chávez and wander east.

    About this map — data sources & caveats

    Tree locations come from three City of Portland Urban Forestry open datasets: the Heritage Trees registry, the Street Tree Inventory, and the Parks Tree Inventory. The three living species of the redwood subfamily load first; five native giants — Douglas-fir, western redcedar, Oregon white oak, bigleaf maple, and ponderosa pine — load when toggled on.

    "Likely predates 1845": Portland's founding year. Trees whose estimated (or documented) age reaches back that far were here before the city — mostly slow-growing Oregon white oaks. The badge is an inference from trunk size, not a measurement.

    Caveat: much of the street inventory was collected by volunteers between 2010 and 2019. Trees grow, and some are gone — treat the map as a very good guide, not gospel. Ages marked "est." are estimated from trunk diameter using species growth rates (no dataset records true age); a documented planting year is shown when the heritage record has one.

    Etiquette: many heritage trees stand on private property. Admire them from the sidewalk.
